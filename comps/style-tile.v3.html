i

What this tile decides

  1. Quiet, not contrasty.Paper carries most of the face; colour lives only in small details (the leaves, the lemons, one wax seal). One soft light from the top-left, short warm shadows, no gradients in the art, nothing tilted.
  2. Real plates, drawn together with ink.Watercolour comes only from public-domain USDA plates; the 1.5 px ink line is what ties them into one hand. The arrangement runs on a diagonal from the top-left corner.
  3. A folded sheet, not an envelope.One sheet of handmade ivory paper, folded once across, closed by a terracotta wax seal with a sprig under it. Terracotta appears in one place only: the wax.
  4. Vertical opening only.My reading of “folded vertically”: the fold runs across the sheet, the top part is folded down and sealed, and on a tap it lifts up on the fold. The invitation then continues downward as the page the guest scrolls. Tell me if you meant a different fold.
  5. Soft paper grain.Fine cotton tooth and gently deckled edges, barely there. The visible handmade grain is shown next to it in grain-compare.html; your pick.
  6. Two type voices.A script for Maya e Tomás only (recommended: Allura, deep olive); EB Garamond for every word a guest must read, at 17 px or more. Whole-card concepts in type-concepts.html.

iv

Ink line

One weight everywhere: 1.5 CSS px, ink #3B2F25, round caps and joins. Drawn with stroke-dashoffset, once; under reduced motion it is simply there.

  • 0.75 px · too faint at arm's length in sunlight
  • 1.5 px · the line · reads as a fine nib on a phone
  • 3 px · reads as a felt marker, fights the watercolour

Watercolours: USDA Pomological Watercolor Collection, public domain, cut out with extract_asset.py (see assets/LOG.md). Shown unfiltered, only rotated, scaled, cropped from the leaf plate and shadowed. Paper grain is drawn in SVG, no image.
