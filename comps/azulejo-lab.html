4 · What I studied

  • Anjo músico com brasão (public domain, Commons)Brushwork: dark cobalt contour, flat diluted wash, white glaze left as highlight, pooled ends, thin grout, painting shifting across tiles.
  • Maritime and country scene, 1725–50 (MNAz)A cut-out (recortado) top edge that follows the painted frame, as on the quinta plaque.
  • 18th-c. panel, vase with flowers (dealer)Plants painted in the same blue as the frame; leaves as soft washes with a darker edge on one side only.
  • Tile with a rocaille ribbon (dealer)How a C-scroll is built: one confident contour, a wash on the shadow side, a volute ending in a pooled dot.
  • Painel de padrão, c. 1590–1620 (MNAz, photo)Pattern tiles where four corners make one flower: the logic of the B3 lining.
  • Palácio Fronteira gardens (TCLF)Blue panels with real green in front: the plant frames the tile, never covers the words.
  • Portugal's blue and white (designer blog)Vines and pots against tiled walls: green and cobalt stay in separate layers, the green casts a soft shadow.
