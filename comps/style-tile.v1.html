i

What this tile decides

  1. One sun, at noon.Every object casts the same short, warm shadow down and to the right, tinted with ink. The page ground is limewash in shade (paper with a breath of azulejo), so sand, lemon and olive read as sunlit. No gradients in the art.
  2. Real plates, drawn together with ink.Watercolour comes only from public-domain USDA plates; the 1.5 px ink line is what ties them into one hand. The arrangement runs on a diagonal from the top-left corner; only the names are centred on anything.
  3. The envelope is material, not decoration.Handmade sand paper with deckled edges and grain, twine-coloured seams, a sprig under the seal. Terracotta appears in one place only: the wax. Blue appears only inside, on the liner.
  4. A script that older guests can read.Recommended: Allura for the names in deep olive, EB Garamond for every word a guest must read, at 17 px or more. Your choice below.

iv

Ink line

One weight everywhere: 1.5 CSS px, ink #3B2F25, round caps and joins. Drawn with stroke-dashoffset, once; under reduced motion it is simply there.

  • 0.75 px · too faint at arm's length in sunlight
  • 1.5 px · the line · reads as a fine nib on a phone
  • 3 px · reads as a felt marker, fights the watercolour

Watercolours: USDA Pomological Watercolor Collection, public domain, cut out with extract_asset.py (see assets/LOG.md). Shown unfiltered, only rotated, scaled, cropped from the leaf plate and shadowed.
